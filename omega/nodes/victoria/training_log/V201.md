## Hypothesis

V200 refuted the carry-injection theory of trend regression (trade
counts identical V199↔V200; suppressor never fired). Re-reading the
trade CSVs across all three gates makes the dominant failure mode
structural and unrelated to carry:

**`crisis_short_bias` is amplifying losing shorts in every snapshot
that contains crisis/high_vol cycles.** Specifically:

- Crisis snap (V200): 36 shorts, 28% WR, avg MAE **−$920**, avg loss
  **−$1,262**, net **−$16,703** (≈86% of the crisis loss).
- Trend snap (V200): 53/106 cycles are labeled `crisis`/`high_vol`,
  so the amplifier fires there too. 46 shorts on trend snap lose
  −$4,382 — that's the V172→V199 trend regression, not carry.
- Recent snap (V200): 36/70 cycles labeled `crisis`/`high_vol`; same
  exposure to the amplifier.

The amplifier stacks four ways: (1) short_thresh × 0.6 at
`strategy.py:1132`, (2) short_thresh × 0.6 *again* at
`strategy.py:1476` (so effective discount is 0.36×, a 64% cut),
(3) short_size × 1.3 at `strategy.py:2816`, (4) the crisis half-Kelly
0.5× safety net **skipped** when `crisis_short_bias` is on
(`strategy.py:2841`). The thesis behind this stack was "be aggressive
shorting sustained crashes"; in practice the crisis snapshot is
snapback-rally-dominated and oversized loose shorts get squeezed.

**V201's bet (one bet, one lever):** neutralise both threshold-side
multipliers (0.6 → 1.0) so crisis shorts require the same conviction
as normal shorts. Keep the long-side 1.5× block (it works — blocks
crisis longs). Keep size multipliers and the kelly skip for now so
the change is purely entry-gate.
