## Hypothesis

V200 refuted the carry-injection theory of trend regression (trade
counts identical V199↔V200; suppressor never fired). Re-reading the
trade CSVs across all three gates makes the dominant failure mode
structural and unrelated to carry:

**`crisis_short_bias` is amplifying losing shorts in every snapshot
that contains crisis/high_vol cycles.** Specifically:

- Crisis snap (V200): 36 shorts, 28% WR, avg MAE **−$920**, avg loss
  **−$1,262**, net **−$16,703** (≈86% of the crisis loss).
- Trend snap (V200): 53/106 cycles are labeled `crisis`/`high_vol`,
  so the amplifier fires there too. 46 shorts on trend snap lose
  −$4,382 — that's the V172→V199 trend regression, not carry.
- Recent snap (V200): 36/70 cycles labeled `crisis`/`high_vol`; same
  exposure to the amplifier.

The amplifier stacks four ways: (1) short_thresh × 0.6 at
`strategy.py:1132`, (2) short_thresh × 0.6 *again* at
`strategy.py:1476` (so effective discount is 0.36×, a 64% cut),
(3) short_size × 1.3 at `strategy.py:2816`, (4) the crisis half-Kelly
0.5× safety net **skipped** when `crisis_short_bias` is on
(`strategy.py:2841`). The thesis behind this stack was "be aggressive
shorting sustained crashes"; in practice the crisis snapshot is
snapback-rally-dominated and oversized loose shorts get squeezed.

**V201's bet (one bet, one lever):** neutralise both threshold-side
multipliers (0.6 → 1.0) so crisis shorts require the same conviction
as normal shorts. Keep the long-side 1.5× block (it works — blocks
crisis longs). Keep size multipliers and the kelly skip for now so
the change is purely entry-gate.

## Conclusion

**Mixed result — hypothesis confirmed for trend, refuted for crisis,
regressed for recent.** The strategic analysis was directionally
correct (the `crisis_short_bias` stack *is* the dominant lever) but
the four amplifications don't all hit the same gate the same way.

Findings:

1. **Trend regression diagnosed.** V200 wrongly blamed carry; V201
   recovers +$5,086 of trend PnL by removing only the threshold
   discount. Trade count effectively unchanged (105 vs 106), short
   count effectively unchanged (45 vs 46), but per-trade quality
   jumped. So the discount was letting low-conviction shorts into
   the `crisis`/`high_vol`-labeled cycles of the trend snapshot, and
   they were the ones dragging trend PnL down. **The V172
   high-water (+$18,437) is now realistically reachable** — V172
   probably had this discount disabled or compensated elsewhere.

2. **Crisis is not a discount problem.** −$18,996 vs −$19,410
   parent — within noise. Same 36 shorts, same MAE profile. The
   crisis P&L damage is in size, not selection — the ×1.3 short /
   ×0.5 long multipliers and the skipped half-Kelly safety net are
   the binding constraints. V202 will pull those.

3. **Recent regressed.** +$223 vs V199's +$2,478. The discount was
   helping NEAR/etc shorts in recent's crisis-labeled cycles — the
   exact cluster V199 had benefited from. So the discount is
   *correct for recent's snapback-rally setups* but *wrong for
   crisis's*. A blanket removal trades recent PnL for trend PnL.

**High-water table:** no records broken. Trend +$12,996 closes the
gap toward V172 but doesn't beat it. README.md unchanged.
