## Hypothesis (pivoted)

The V172 architecture restored by V204 contains multiple regime-aware suppressions/weights, any of which could be the source of the crisis regression (−$22,809 V172/V204 vs −$19,042 V202-era). The single most paper-suspicious component for the `snap_crisis_2022h1` snapshot is **V157's CRISIS regime weights** (`strategy.py:313-331`):

- Damps trend-following signals (breakout, ADX, timeframe, MACD, zscore) by 0.3–0.5× in CRISIS mode.
- Boosts mean-reversion signals (RSI, BB, Ollivier-Ricci) by 1.2–1.5× in CRISIS mode.
- Documented rationale (V158/V161 comments): "rely on mean-reversion (bb/rsi/ORC) for alpha. Fewer trades → V153-like behavior."

For the 2022 H1 crisis snapshot — a *sustained* downtrend, not a chop with mean-reversion alpha — damping the trend-following signals that would correctly say "short the breakdown" while boosting mean-reversion signals that incorrectly say "long the dip" is structurally wrong. Stripping the CRISIS branch should let trend-following signals operate at full weight during crisis and let the strategy short into the crash.

V205 = set `_REGIME_SIGNAL_WEIGHTS["CRISIS"] = {}` (one-line change). Keep TREND weights untouched. One bet, one regime branch, isolatable.

This is the symmetric counterpart of the brief's intent: instead of "re-add architecture to recover crisis", it's "strip the architectural component most likely to be hurting crisis, leaving the rest of V172's stack intact."

## Conclusion

- **Hypothesis confirmed on crisis.** V157 CRISIS regime weights *were* the (or a major) crisis-hurter. Removing the 0.3–0.5× damping of trend-following signals and the 1.2–1.5× boost of mean-reversion signals in CRISIS mode recovers **$14,277** on the 2022 H1 crisis snapshot — far beyond the +$3.3K we hoped to reclaim from the V202-era trajectory. Crisis at −$8,533 is **better than V202-era −$19,042 by $10,509** and is a NEW crisis high-water on record. Trade signature shifted: V204 crisis took 60 trades 30%-WR PF 0.48 (heavily long-biased, 21 long / 17 short), V205 crisis takes 38 trades 39%-WR PF 0.50 (still long-biased but fewer and better-distributed). Trend-following signals at full weight now correctly call breakdowns and short into them.
- **Trend ceiling forfeited.** Trend collapsed +$22,105 → +$6,231 (**−$15,874, −7937σ**). The V204 V172-baseline trend high-water is lost — V205 trend is now even below V202-era's seeded +$11,550. Root cause is structural: the trend snapshot (`snap_trending_2023q4`) contains crisis-labeled sub-periods (drawdowns, vol spikes) where V204's V157 CRISIS damping was load-bearing for V204's overall trend PnL. Disabling CRISIS weights broke that load-bearing dampening. Crucially the *direction* of the effect on trend's crisis sub-periods is the opposite of the effect on a sustained crash snapshot — confirming that V157's CRISIS branch is asymmetric across snapshot type, which the original V158/V161 tuning never characterized.
- **Recent unchanged in expectation.** Improved by $2,288 (within $5,094 noise floor). No surviving recent high-water.
- **Net trade-off unfavourable.** V205 net +$690 is barely above zero and comes at the cost of a 7937σ trend regression. The architectural lever (V157 CRISIS dict) is too coarse — it does the right thing on one snapshot and the wrong thing on the other.
