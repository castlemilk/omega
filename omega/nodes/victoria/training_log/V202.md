V201 isolated `crisis_short_bias` as the cause of the V172→V199 trend
regression by neutralising the threshold discount (recovered ~60% of
the trend gap). Crisis was barely changed (−$18,996 vs −$19,410),
which **falsified the selection theory** and points the next bet at
sizing: in `crisis`/`high_vol` cycles V201 still amplifies shorts
×1.3, scales longs ×0.5, and skips the crisis half-Kelly 0.5× safety
net. Snapback rallies on crisis snaps then squeeze oversized loose
shorts.

**V202's bet (one crisis-sizing bet, bundled):** stop amplifying short
size in crisis cycles and let Kelly cap risk normally.

1. Drop the post-weight size block at `strategy.py:2814-2828` (shorts
   ×1.3, longs ×0.5).
2. Restore the crisis half-Kelly 0.5× safety net at
   `strategy.py:2842-2851` by removing the
   `and not self.features.crisis_short_bias` guard so crisis cycles
   always get the 0.5× scaling regardless of the feature flag.

These are two edits but the same hypothesis ("crisis sizing is too
aggressive"). Bundling is intentional. **If results are ambiguous
(e.g. crisis improves but recent regresses), V203 splits this into
V203a (size-amp removal alone) + V203b (kelly restore alone) to
attribute which half mattered.** If crisis stays < −$15K both edits

## Conclusion

**Hypothesis refuted.** Crisis sizing is NOT the binding constraint
for crisis P&L. Bundled change moved nothing on crisis (−$19,003 vs
−$18,996 parent — within noise), and the half-Kelly restoration
slightly hurt trend (−$4,166) by scaling down crisis-labeled trend
cycles that were profitable in V201.

Findings:

1. **Crisis is structurally exit-side, not sizing.** Same 63 trades,
   same per-trade losses across V201 → V202 with two different
   size regimes. Crisis short losses survive both ×1.3 amplification
   (V201) and ×0.5 half-Kelly damping (V202) at the same magnitude
   — meaning the loss per dollar exposed isn't changing. That's the
   signature of an exit-side problem: trades go in at correct(-ish)
   size but bleed out via stops/trails that don't fit snapback
   regime. V203 = crisis trail tightener.

2. **Trend regression localises the half-Kelly skip's value.**
   V202's trend drop traces to the half-Kelly being re-applied to
   crisis-labeled cycles in the trend snapshot — the same cycles
   that V201's threshold-discount removal had cleaned up. So the
   `crisis_short_bias`-gated half-Kelly skip wasn't a safety hole
   in V201, it was load-bearing for trend in cycles where the
   discount removal had already tightened selection. V203 should
   **revert the half-Kelly restoration** (keep V201's behaviour
   there) and instead target crisis exits specifically.

3. **Recent unchanged.** Confirms recent is dominated by NEAR
   cluster behaviour that V201's threshold removal already broke;
   sizing changes don't touch it. The +$2,478 V199 high-water needs
   V203's threshold-regime gating (the original V203 conditional
   path) — but that's secondary to fixing crisis.

4. **Bundle attribution:** crisis flat in both halves of the bundle,
   so splitting V203a/V203b would not have been informative. The
   bundle was the right call; the hypothesis was just wrong.

**High-water table:** no records broken. Trend regressed to V200-
level (+$8,830 vs V200 +$7,910), still well below V172 (+$18,437).
README.md `_trend best_ unchanged, crisis still no positive run.
