## §1 — What was asked, and what the data allows

The brief: fork a new ASX system following Victoria's approach, pulling in **news,
announcements and other metadata** to form a perspective.

Before building that pipeline I measured whether the metadata is *historically*
available, because a perspective you cannot backtest is not a perspective. On the free
path (yfinance, already a dependency):

| Source | What you get | Backtestable? |
|---|---|---|
| `Ticker.news` | ~10 **recent** items (title, pubDate, STORY/VIDEO) | **No** — no history |
| `Ticker.calendar` | Ex-dividend and earnings dates, **forward-looking** | **No** |
| `Ticker.info` | sector, industry, marketCap, trailingPE, dividendYield, beta, institutional holdings | **No** — point-in-time snapshot of *today* |
| `Ticker.history` | daily OHLCV, split- and dividend-adjusted, 10y+ | **Yes** |

**Only the bars are historical.** Backtesting an announcement strategy on the other
three would mean either no data or pure lookahead — using today's PE and sector to make
a 2020 decision. This is the same blocker that paused V259 (Polymarket: "no frozen
crypto-binary probability series exists"), and it is better to hit it now than after
building the pipeline.

**Consequence for the architecture:** the loader freezes what is genuinely historical
and leaves a typed seam for metadata that must be *acquired with history*. The project
YAML declares no metadata node, and says why.

### The ASX-specific metadata that IS worth acquiring

Not built, listed so the acquisition decision is informed:

- **ASX company announcements** — mandatory continuous disclosure, timestamped, and
  carrying a *price-sensitive* flag. Structurally far better than the global news
  sentiment that V260 refuted for crypto: company-specific, dated, and pre-classified.
  Bulk history is the hard part (vendor or a properly archived scrape).
- **ASIC daily short positions** — published per stock, free, and genuinely historical.
  The single best free ASX-specific metadata source, and the cheapest to acquire.
- **Delisted-inclusive index membership** — not a signal but the **blocking input** for
  V286 §5. Without it no ASX result can be trusted.

## §2 — What was built

`omega/nodes/asx/` — a project node following the `on_chain_flow` / `intraday_alpha`
shape, importing nothing from platform code (CLAUDE.md separation).

- `loader.py` — freeze / verify / load, with the **V219 manifest discipline from day
  one** rather than bolted on after four versions of pain:
  - `freeze_universe()` is the **only** function that touches the network;
  - `verify_frozen_manifest()` md5s every frozen file;
  - `load_frozen_bars(strict=True)` verifies **by default** and raises on drift.
    Victoria's equivalent check was opt-in, and an undeclared substrate change cost
    V276→V282 four versions to diagnose.
- `ASXUniverse` records `constructed_at` and `survivorship_safe`, so the bias travels
  **in the artifact** rather than in someone's memory. The starter universe is flagged
  `survivorship_safe=False` with a note pointing at V286 §5.
- `projects/asx.yaml` — declares the data layer only; the "not yet declared, and why"
  section is deliberate.

**Verified:** 20 tickers frozen, 2,531 bars each (2016-08→2026-08), manifest CLEAN.
Positive control — tamper one byte and `load_frozen_bars` aborts naming the file and
both hashes; restore and it returns to CLEAN. The guard can fail, which per V275's
lesson is the only reason a passing guard means anything.

## §3 — What was deliberately NOT built

**No signal node. No strategy node. No flag.**

V286 §3 found MA-crossover mean-reverts on ASX large caps in all twelve cells tested,
OOS-stable, with implied edges of 63–109 bps against ~20 bps costs. That is the most
promising result in weeks — and V286 §5 states why it may be worthless: the universe is
**today's** large caps, and for a *reversion* finding survivorship bias manufactures
exactly that signal. Names that fell and kept falling were delisted and are absent.

Building a signal node on it now would be constructing a mechanism around a number that
one data acquisition could erase. This session has the precedents: V283 measured a
genuinely better forecaster with no consumer; V284 built the consumer and it lost money;
V285's most exciting table evaporated under a train/test split.

## §4 — Next steps

1. **Acquire delisted-inclusive ASX constituent history and re-run V286 §3.** The single
   blocking test. It needs no strategy code and it decides whether this project has a
   thesis or a selection artifact.
2. **If it survives** — portfolio backtest with realistic ASX costs (~20 bps round trip),
   long-only *and* long/short variants, because retail shorting constraints may remove
   half of a demeaned strategy by construction (V286 §1).
3. **ASIC short positions** are the cheapest genuinely-historical ASX metadata and the
   natural first metadata node once (1) clears.
4. **Announcements** last, and only with real history. The price-sensitive flag makes
   them a far better-posed problem than V260's refuted news-sentiment work — but that is
   an argument for acquiring them properly, not for approximating them from recent-only
   feeds.
